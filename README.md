# Gambaga-Humanitarian-Health-Outreach
Humanitarian healthcare, community engagement and health equity in Gambaga, Ghana — October 2026.


Portfolio author: Dr. Emmanuel Kwame Akuamoah, MBChB  
Location: Gambaga, North East Region, Ghana  
Period: October 2026  
Collection: 32 photographs and 8 videos across eight themes
1. Project overview

This portfolio documents a humanitarian health outreach in Gambaga, including engagement with residents of the Gambaga Outcast Home(women and children accused of witchcraft) and the surrounding community. The initiative brought clinical services, health education and material assistance closer to people facing barriers to healthcare. It illustrates the intersection of clinical medicine, community partnership and public health practice.

2. Humanitarian context

Social exclusion, stigma, financial constraints and geographical barriers can compound difficulties in obtaining timely healthcare. The outreach provided an opportunity to understand these challenges in their local context, while recognizing the dignity, agency and experiences of community members. Residential photographs provide environmental context rather than evidence of any individual’s socioeconomic or medical status.

3. Objectives

-Facilitate access to basic clinical assessment, screening and appropriate treatment.

-Promote preventive health practices through community education.

-Engage traditional leadership and local healthcare personnel to support culturally respectful service delivery.

-Provide essential supplies and address barriers to healthcare access.

4. My role

As organizer, team lead and volunteer physician, I conceived and coordinated the outreach with local healthcare personnel, engaged traditional leadership, established a consultation point, assessed patients and prescribed treatment where indicated. I also contributed to health education, medication donations and discussions about discrimination and healthcare-seeking behavior. These responsibilities reflect my account of the outreach; the media below illustrate said activities

5. Stakeholder engagement and implementation

The outreach began with engagement with traditional authorities, followed by community mobilization and clinical activities. Working alongside local healthcare workers, the team organized consultations, basic screening and distribution of supplies. Community visits provided additional opportunities to listen and understand the setting in which residents live. Health education included hand hygiene and malaria prevention.

6. Documented outputs and impact

The outreach screened 141 individuals: 87 women and 40 children from the Gambaga Outcast Home, and 14 members of the surrounding community. These outreach figures were verified by the Medical Superintendent of Gambaga.
Among the 87 women screened, 29 (33.3%) had elevated blood pressure readings. Further clinical inquiries identified stigma-related barriers to obtaining medication refills at the nearby health facility. The team provided on-site antihypertensive medication refills from donated supplies and engaged the surrounding community in discussions about discrimination and healthcare-seeking behavior.
The collection also documents clinical consultations, finger-prick testing, blood pressure assessment, material handovers and residential visits. The screening figures and immediate interventions are distinct from longer-term clinical outcomes.

7. Public health reflection

This experience reinforced my conviction that healthcare should be a right rather than a privilege. Clinical outreach can address immediate needs, but lasting improvements require accessible primary care, reliable referral pathways, equitable resource allocation and policies that address social exclusion. I want to build healthcare systems capable of reaching communities I may never have the opportunity to visit. Pursuing graduate training in public health is a means of translating these experiences into evidence-informed, sustainable interventions.

8. Ethics and responsible use

Informed verbal consent was obtained from participants and the facility caretaker for the outreach documentation. This portfolio is intended for professional and academic review, with respect for participant dignity, confidentiality and the agreed scope of consent. The media should not be redistributed beyond that scope. Video descriptions are based on sampled visual frames.
