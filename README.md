## 1. Executive Summary

This portfolio documents a humanitarian health outreach in Gambaga, including engagement with residents of the Gambaga Outcast Home, which accommodates women accused of witchcraft and children living with them, as well as members of the surrounding community.

The initiative brought clinical services, preventive health education and material assistance closer to individuals experiencing barriers to healthcare. Through collaboration with traditional authorities and local healthcare personnel, the outreach sought to address immediate healthcare needs while exploring the broader social and structural determinants of health.

The initiative illustrates the intersection of clinical medicine, humanitarian service, community partnership, health equity and public health practice.

## 2. Background and Humanitarian Context

Social exclusion, stigma, financial constraints and geographical barriers can compound difficulties in obtaining timely healthcare.

For marginalized populations, the consequences of these barriers extend beyond access to medical consultations and medications. They can influence preventive healthcare utilization, continuity of treatment and overall health outcomes.

The outreach provided an opportunity to understand these challenges within their local context while recognizing the dignity, agency and lived experiences of community members.

Residential photographs included in this portfolio provide environmental and contextual documentation rather than evidence of any individual's socioeconomic or medical status.

## 3. Project Objectives

- Facilitate access to basic clinical assessment, screening and appropriate treatment.
- Promote preventive health practices through community health education.
- Engage traditional leadership and local healthcare personnel to support culturally respectful service delivery.
- Identify barriers to healthcare access and continuity of treatment.
- Provide essential medications, humanitarian supplies and material assistance.
- Promote community awareness of discrimination and its implications for healthcare-seeking behavior.

## 4. Documented Outputs and Key Outcomes

| Indicator | Documented Output |
| --- | ---: |
| Total individuals screened | 141 |
| Women from the Gambaga Outcast Home | 87 |
| Children from the Gambaga Outcast Home | 40 |
| Members of the surrounding community | 14 |
| Women with elevated blood pressure readings | 29 |
| Proportion of women with elevated blood pressure readings | 33.3% |

**Verification:** The outreach screening figures were verified by the Medical Superintendent of Gambaga.

### Clinical Findings and Immediate Interventions

Among the 87 women screened, 29 (33.3%) had elevated blood pressure readings.

Further clinical inquiries identified stigma-related barriers to obtaining medication refills at the nearby health facility. In response, the outreach team provided on-site antihypertensive medication refills from donated supplies.

The team also engaged members of the surrounding community in discussions about discrimination and its implications for healthcare-seeking behavior.

Additional activities included:

- Clinical consultations and assessments.
- Blood pressure screening.
- Finger-prick and point-of-care testing.
- Prescription and provision of medications where indicated.
- Preventive health education.
- Distribution of essential humanitarian supplies.
- Community and residential visits to educate residents on stigma and other barriers to healthcare access

The screening figures and immediate interventions are distinct from longer-term clinical outcomes.

## 7. Follow-Up and Sustainability

The outreach highlighted the need to move beyond episodic humanitarian interventions toward sustainable healthcare delivery.

Although the initiative addressed immediate clinical and humanitarian needs, long-term improvements require continued collaboration with local healthcare personnel, accessible primary healthcare services and interventions that address discrimination and treatment access barriers.

Key priorities for sustained intervention include:

- Continuity of care for individuals requiring ongoing treatment.
- Reliable access to essential medications.
- Appropriate referral pathways.
- Community education addressing stigma and discrimination.
- Strengthened collaboration between healthcare providers and community stakeholders.
- Integration of marginalized populations into routine healthcare services.


## 8. Public Health Reflection

This experience reinforced my conviction that healthcare should be a right rather than a privilege.

While clinical outreach can address immediate healthcare needs, lasting improvements require accessible primary care, reliable referral pathways, equitable resource allocation and policies that address social exclusion.

The identification of treatment access barriers among women with elevated blood pressure readings illustrated how social determinants can influence clinical outcomes and continuity of care.

It also strengthened my understanding that effective healthcare delivery requires more than clinical expertise. It requires community partnership, effective health systems, evidence-informed policies and a commitment to equity.

I want to help build healthcare systems capable of reaching communities I may never have the opportunity to visit.

Pursuing graduate training in public health represents an opportunity to translate these experiences into evidence-informed, sustainable interventions that address the structural barriers affecting underserved populations.
